# Voice guide: how I talk upstream

## Who I am in threads

I'm a new contributor working through a AI course who does not have much open-source experience yet. On any issue I touch, I'm there to reproduce, investigate, or report back, but not yet to promise a merge, and I want a maintainer to trust my next comment exactly as much as my last one.

## Rules I write by

### Rule: No promised timelines

I never promise an specific date to fix or add something. I promise a
concrete next step instead, and I let the actual work set the pace.

- Wrong: "I will fix this by tomorrow, promise!!"
- Right: "Repro report on the way; I'll follow up with what I find in
  the code path."

### Rule: Name the version and the behavior

Every claim comment names the specific version and the specific
behavior, never say it in a generic way such as "this bug,".

- Wrong: "I'm seeing this bug too, can confirm."
- Right: "v1.20.0 ignores --style exactly as described, reproduced
  offline, report below."

### Rule: No bot voice

I write the way I'd say it out loud to a person.

- Wrong: "Amazing project!! Please assign me this issue, I'd love to
  help!!"
- Right: "Picking this up as a first contribution, naming the
  version/behavior above."

### Rule: Flag being new at contribution

When something is my first contribution to a project, I say so up
front. An honest flag reads better to a maintainer than false
confidence does.

- Wrong: "I've got this, no problem."
- Right: "This is my first contribution here, flagging that up front."

## Things I never post

- A guaranteed fix date, or "guaranteed" anything about an outcome I
  have not yet produced.
- A simple "same here" or "any updates?" with nothing of my own
  attached.
- Confident diagnosis language ("verified," "100%")
  over a result I have not actually shown in the same comment.
